# Cheri's style rules

Read at the start of every video. Add a dated line when he confirms or rejects something.

## Always
- One vertical 9:16 video per request. No 16:9, no alternate versions, no licence caveats in reports unless he asks.
- Every video ends with "Edit by Cheri" (end card or final seconds).
- Use the pictures and clips he supplies as they are.

## Look
- 2026-11-25, Work Showcase notes: he prefers a beautiful photograph as the background (soft nature pictures he picks from
  Pinterest) to flat paper-white or hard colour fields (yellow, green, blue slides). Ask for or reuse his background
  pictures; text in deep ink with the key word in cream.
- He describes himself as a "product designer and software engineer" (not "I build websites"). He also does branding
  (logos, variants, brand boards, mock-ups), exported from Figma; show it next to the websites.
- Loved: the Vox documentary paper look: warm paper, taped cut-outs, red stamps, yellow highlighter, maps with a
  camera following the route. A story told in first person, with flow, not a list of points.
- Rejected: dark neon or purple palettes, generic floating 3D shapes and grids, slide-by-slide lists.
- Fill the frame: pictures full-bleed with depth, dense content, little empty space.
- The camera goes with the action: continuous, curved, never fully stopping. His favourite kind of motion.
- Funny meme pictures (cats at laptops) are welcome as beats when the tone allows.
- Never reuse an earlier video's pictures, screenshots or signature scenes; same style, new parts each time.
- His own picture only as a tiny round avatar, never large. More real-world pictures in general.

## Pace and voice
- Fast pacing: no added pauses between scenes; trim the voice's own long silences to about a quarter second. Hold only
  where the viewer must read or screenshot.
- Narration, when used, is lively and a little fast (about +15 to +20 %).
- Amharic versions: short on-screen words only, no subtitles, no narration; he records his own voice over it.

## Sound
- 2026-11-25: in a row of like scenes (the four sites) picture and sound must "rock together" exactly: put the gestures on
  the straight hits (kick on one, claps on two and four), not on syncopated ones, start each move at full strength on its
  frame, and use sounds with an instant attack (`camera-shutter`, `click-soft`, `stamp-thunk`); `shutter-soft` and
  `marker-stroke` peak late and sound like a flam against a drum. (The cause is my diagnosis; he has not yet confirmed the fix.)
- 2026-11-25, the beat-grid test video ("wow... save that for every video editing. super good"): the beat grid is the
  default for every video. Plan the sound first with the `beat-grid` skill (`studio grid`): a tempo where a sixteenth is
  whole frames, every cut, word and sound on a frame that is a hit, one gesture per hit, one beat of silence before the
  big moment, hard cuts, the mix at -14 LUFS. What he saw and liked: dry percussion made by `studio grid track`, one word
  per hit in Oswald with the key word in red Playfair italic on paper, a 32-slide drop, the credit on the last hit.
  For videos led by his voice or by footage with its own sound, keep the voice in charge and put the cuts, captions and
  effects on the grid under it (my reading; he did not spell this case out).
- Music: the grid percussion track (above) or a track he supplies; no generic stock music. Tactile sound effects on the hits.
- Vary the sounds (clicks, taps, stamp, paper slide, page turn, pen, typewriter keys, plucks, tiny bell); never the
  same one twice in a row. Whooshes are rare and never on text.

## Captions
- Vary them from line to line (size, emphasis, entrance); never the same pill on every line.

## Working together
- Use both graphics engines in one video, each for what it does best (2026-11-22).
- Paid tools only after the cost is stated and he says yes.

## Footage pieces (mood cuts from his own clips)
- 2026-11-22, Night Shift: "very nice", and he prefers the version with text: three short lines landing on shots, a clean
  sans with the last word in an italic serif, cream on the dark footage. Natural sound plus a few quiet effects, no music.
- 2026-11-22, Night Shift second pass: effects belong across the whole video, not in one scene. Graphics that go behind him
  stay above and around his head (cut-outs are weak below the neck). Drop the camera's own sound: a cinematic bed or none,
  since he may add a trending TikTok sound. Level tilted shots and steady handheld ones before cutting.
- 2026-11-22, Night Shift third pass: he loved the lights-off scene (a hard change with one clear sound). Mixed tactile
  sounds on a row of cards sounded "awful" to him: when several like things appear in a row, use one simple click, the
  same each time. Typing should sound like real keys. No music on this kind of piece; a low room drone is the most.
- 2026-11-23: he hates the typed prompt-bar component ("make it cinematic" typing in a pill with a send button). Do not use it.
  He also cut the ending beats (peace sign, walk to camera): end on the strongest shot, with the credit inside it.
- 2026-11-23: a cut-out of a dark figure in a dark room tears wherever a dark limb crosses a bright graphic, and he notices.
  Let graphics pass behind the head only (a clean edge), keep them clear of arms and body, and say so before relying on a cut-out.
- 2026-11-23, Before You Win (vlog to a speech track), his notes after a busy cut: too many texts on screen at once; the
  transitions were bad (zoom-ins landing on every cut, white flashes): use plain hard cuts; no background colour behind
  subtitles or text (no highlight blocks, tags, chips or bubbles); "make it professional". So: one text at a time, in one
  place, plain white with at most one accent-colour word; cuts are hard cuts. Open on him (walking, face), not on scenery.
  Keep saturation a little under natural: boosted colour shows on his skin.
- 2026-11-23, correction to the note above: he LIKES the 3D text moves and hand-drawn illustrations ("they were cool") and was
  angry when they were removed. A note about clutter means fewer things at once, not no effects: change only what he
  named and keep the rest. Captions: one or two words at a time, switching with the speech, a bit smaller than feels
  natural (about 150 px capitals / 76 px italic at 1080 wide), no background, clear of his face.
- 2026-11-23: the 3D he means is text set INSIDE the scene, lying on a real surface in perspective and moving with it (the
  words painted on the road), not words spinning in the air. Mix styles in one video: in-scene text, a rotating 3D carousel
  of clip cards, Vox paper moments (taped photos, red stamp, highlighter). Cuts: do not cut back to nearly the same moment
  of the same clip; return to a clip seconds later or go to a different scene, so each cut shows something new.
